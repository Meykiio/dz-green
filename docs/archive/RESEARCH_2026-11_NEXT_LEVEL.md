# Make survival, not plantings, Green Algeria's product

The thing most likely to make Green Algeria worth adopting is not another map layer. It is becoming the one public place in Algeria that shows **which planted trees are still alive**, checked by someone other than the planter, with the method published next to every number. Nobody does this today. The DGF reports hectares and plant counts. One-day campaigns report totals that disagree with each other. Survival figures of 95 to 98% appear with no method. The biggest finding in the notes changes how you should pitch the project: **the state already has a reforestation-tracking platform**. UNDP built "Tashdjir" with the DGF for its 58 forest conservations, and the World Bank is designing a national fire early-warning and data system with a pilot in Bejaia. So Green Algeria cannot sell itself as "the platform". It has to be the citizen and volunteer layer that feeds those systems. Three things stand between you and any government deal. Your current hosting (Vercel plus Supabase, outside Algeria) almost certainly breaks Law 18-07 as amended by Law 25-11 for a state deployment, and it is a compliance gap even today. A solo individual cannot sign a public contract, so you need a company with the Startup label. And the name "Green Algeria" collides with "Algerie Verte", the association the Agriculture Ministry already co-runs national planting days with. The good news is that most of the technical upgrades (better fire-model inputs, a lighter map, survival check-ins) are free, open-data work a solo developer can do in weeks.

## A state platform already exists, so be the layer that feeds it

The single most important fact for your strategy: **UNDP, with the DGF, built "Tashdjir"**, a platform that lets the DGF and its 58 conservations manage, track and monitor replanting with geographic data. It has modules for reforestation sites, species, seed stands, nurseries and impact, and covered 185,400 seedlings on about 1,400 ha in a project budgeted at **USD 872,315** ([UNDP Algeria](https://www.undp.org/fr/algeria/actualites/undps-commitment-support-algerias-forest-rehabilitation); [UNDP project document](https://info.undp.org/docs/pdc/Documents/DZA/Prodoc%20Feux%20de%20For%C3%AAts_Sign%C3%A9.pdf)). The notes could not confirm whether Tashdjir is still live, where it is hosted, or whether it accepts citizen input. Find that out before you pitch anyone. Meanwhile the World Bank's PROGREEN work found that Algeria's forest **data systems are fragmented**. It is building an investment plan to scale up nationwide early warning, with a Bejaia pilot, and notes that the 2023 Forestry Law and 2024 Disaster Risk Management Law promote community participation ([World Bank, June 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2026/06/17/from-fire-to-resilience-rethinking-how-algeria-manages-its-forests)). Smaller initiatives also exist. A national forest-fire monitoring cell was installed in July 2026, and Mascara wilaya launched its own fire-tracking platform in August 2026 ([Radio Algerie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/en/node/90996)).

The state is also consolidating. The High Commission for Digitalisation is pulling public services into one "Dzair Services" portal ([We Are Tech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/algeria-launches-dzair-services-to-centralize-public-digital-platforms)). In July 2026 the President gave administrations **one month to interconnect their systems through the National Data Center** ([We Are Tech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/algeria-sets-one-month-deadline-for-government-data-center-integration)). The direction is clear: a new standalone government portal is unlikely to be bought. A clean data feed, an API and a verified citizen layer that plugs into what exists are far more likely to be valued.

The rest of the context backs the same positioning. The Barrage Vert was relaunched on 29 October 2023 with **over 75 billion DZD phased to 2030**. The DGF reports **42,376 ha planted for 2023-2026** but only **9,083 ha "effectively developed"**, and 47,917 ha (38.8 million plants) is programmed for 2026-2027 ([Algerie360](https://www.algerie360.com/dgf-reboisement-barrage-vert-2026-2027/); [Algerie-Eco](https://algerie-eco.com/2026/10/23/rehabilitation-du-barrage-vert-plus-de-42-000-hectares-plantes-entre-2023-et-2026/)). That gap between "planted" and "established" shows the state itself knows the difference, and survival tracking is exactly the missing piece. International money is arriving too. The Green Climate Fund approved **FP310, USD 99.5M over seven years with FAO as the accredited entity**, across six wilayas ([GCF](https://www.greenclimate.fund/portfolio/projects/fp310)). Projects like that have years of monitoring and reporting duties. Treat that as a possible channel, not a confirmed budget line.

Fire reporting to the state today is **phone only**: 1070 for forests (launched June 2022) and 1021 for Protection Civile ([Radio Algerie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/10968)). Forest fire prevention and forest management sit with the DGF under the Ministry of Agriculture. Firefighting sits with Protection Civile under the Interior Ministry. A National Forest Protection Commission was installed in March 2026 ([Algerie-Eco](https://algerie-eco.com/2026/03/14/la-commission-nationale-de-protection-des-forets-pour-lannee-2026-installee/)). Your plausible buyer is therefore the **DGF or a wilaya Conservation des forets**, not Protection Civile, which has its own command chain. Keep routing people to the official numbers. The notes found no government app for citizen fire reports or tree tracking, but absence in search results is not proof that none exists.

## Survival tracking is the feature every successful program shares

Across every big campaign the notes covered, the pattern is the same. **Official survival figures run 80 to 98%, while independent or local checks find 15 to 40%.** Turkey planted about 11 million saplings in one day in 2019. The ministry claimed 95% rooting, and the forestry union claimed up to 90% died from wrong-season planting ([Duvar English](https://www.duvarenglish.com/amp/90-pct-of-11-mln-tree-saplings-planted-by-govt-died-news-11027)). Ethiopia claims 84 to 85%, but a district study found **39.8%** over 2019-2024 ([Ecoticias](https://www.ecoticias.com/en/ethiopia-put-805-million-seedlings-in-the-ground-in-twelve-hours-with-26-million-people-helping-and-the-number-that-will-actually-decide-whether-it-worked-is-still-years-away/35700/)). India's 2026 audit found the Green India Mission reached about **5% of its afforestation target** despite geotagging and drones ([Mongabay India](https://india.mongabay.com/2026/08/audit-exposes-fundamental-flaws-in-green-india-missions-execution/)). China's Three-North shelterbelt, the closest match to the Barrage Vert, has an estimated **15% long-term survival** in arid zones ([Cao 2008](https://cdn.greensoft.mn/uploads/users/1277/files/Greenmongolia/%D0%93%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%B0%D0%B4/Mongolian_steppe_studies/Article_Gadaad_PDF/Cao_afforestationfailure_EnvSciTech_2008.pdf)). Algeria has its own history here. A 2010 study of the national reforestation plan found cork-oak plantings with no more than 10% success ([CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/15516886.pdf)), and the 1970s Djelfa pine belts declined from grazing, logging and the pine processionary caterpillar ([CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/33522326.pdf)).

The causes are known. In Mediterranean climates **the first summer drought is the main killer of seedlings** ([Vallejo et al. 2012](https://zenodo.org/record/11004/files/2012_Vallejo_et_al_NewForest.pdf)). Wet-season planting and planter training raise survival ([Ecoticias](https://www.ecoticias.com/en/ethiopia-put-805-million-seedlings-in-the-ground-in-twelve-hours-with-26-million-people-helping-and-the-number-that-will-actually-decide-whether-it-worked-is-still-years-away/35700/)). Planting under nurse shrubs helps most on dry, sunny slopes ([Gomez-Aparicio meta-analysis](https://ecologia.ugr.es/sites/dpto/ecologia/public/inline-images/Applying-plant-facilitation.pdf)). Programs that count planting instead of survival, and run for three years or less, fail ([Yale E360](https://e360.yale.edu/features/phantom-forests-tree-planting-climate-change)). The 2025 Algerian one-day campaign planted roughly 1.3 to 1.4 million trees. Observers said the hard part is maintaining them, not planting them ([Swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/fre/succ%C3%A8s-en-alg%C3%A9rie-pour-la-plantation-d%27un-million-d%27arbres-en-24-h/90228830)).

The serious platforms copy each other on the fix, and you can copy it cheaply. **TreeMapper** (Plant-for-the-Planet, open source) stores remeasurements as a timeline per tree and **highlights trees due for a check on the map** ([TreeMapper FAQ](https://treemapper-documentation.pages.dev/docs/faq)). **Ecosia** monitors for at least three years, counts small sites tree by tree and **samples large sites** ([Ecosia support](https://support.ecosia.org/article/362-monitoring)). **WRI's TerraMatch** counts at baseline, mid-project and year 5, and reports volunteers and species per site ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/monitoring-locally-led-land-restoration-scale/)). **Restor** requires name, type, status and start date before a site goes public, and groups sites into a portfolio view ([Restor help](https://intercom.help/restor/en/articles/11769861-understanding-site-types-and-intervention-types-on-restor)). **iNaturalist** gives a record "Research Grade" only with metadata, a photo and agreement from more than two thirds of identifiers, and users can vote it back down ([iNaturalist help](https://help.inaturalist.org/support/solutions/articles/151000169936)). Berlin's open-source **Giess den Kiez** proves that aftercare can be crowdsourced: volunteers adopt street trees and log their waterings ([Technologiestiftung](https://technologiestiftung-berlin.de/en/projects/giess-den-kiez)).

Put together, the feature set is: a campaign or site record with required fields that a group (school, association, company) owns for years, not for one event; **survival check-ins at about 3, 12 and 36 months**, with a status (alive, stressed, dead, burned, grazed) and a repeat photo from the same spot; reminders, with overdue sites shown on the map; a server-chosen random sample for big campaigns, since a random sample is harder to fake than self-selected trees; and quality grades on every record (unverified, community-confirmed, moderator-verified). Survival rate goes on the home page and the wilaya pages, **with the sample size and method next to it**. Simple anti-fraud checks fit here too: EXIF time and location against the submitted point, duplicate-photo detection, and a cap on trees per submission. Skip blockchain. An append-only audit table in Postgres gives the same tamper-evidence, and Veritree's own blockchain only proves a record was not changed, not that the tree exists ([Veritree](https://www.veritree.com/why-veritree/how-it-works)).

Be careful with gamification. The evidence is thin, and leaderboards sometimes **lower** performance and demotivate people once one player is far ahead ([arXiv 1707.03704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03704); [Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/419303/1/ReevesWestSimperlHComp18.pdf)). If you add any, reward survival and care (trees alive at one year, waterings logged, check-ins done), compare like with like (school against school inside a wilaya), and always show people the overall result. Never reward raw planting counts. That is the metric failed campaigns chased.

For fire, two patterns are worth taking. From **Global Forest Watch**: **area-of-interest subscriptions** (pick a commune or draw a polygon, get alerts) and a field loop where people confirm or reject each satellite alert ([GFW blog](https://blog.globalforestwatch.org/features/introducing-my-gfw-a-customized-forest-monitoring-experience); [WRI on Forest Watcher](https://www.wri.org/insights/forest-watcher-brings-data-straight-environmental-defenders)). A FIRMS hotspot marked "confirmed / false alarm / out" by a local is verified data a government does not have. From **Watch Duty** and **Fogos.pt**: put a **"last updated" time and a source label on every item**, and have humans write short incident summaries. Watch Duty's rule is that "a human writes every alert" ([Watch Duty 2025 report](https://www.watchduty.org/blog/2025-annual-report)). Fogos.pt grew from a volunteer side project into national infrastructure by republishing civil-protection data under a cooperation protocol, and it survived DDoS attacks behind a CDN ([Fogos.pt](https://fogos.pt/en/sobre); [Cloudflare](https://ghost.blog.cloudflare.com/tag/portugal/)). Watch Duty also teaches a business lesson: its paying "pro" customers are **utilities, telecoms and railways**, not governments ([Watch Duty 2025 report](https://www.watchduty.org/blog/2025-annual-report)). In Algeria the equivalents would be Sonelgaz, Algerie Telecom and SNTF. That is an untested inference, but it is a more realistic first revenue than a ministry.

## Free satellite data can upgrade both the fire model and "Where to plant"

Your current 2027 priority map is trained on FIRMS hotspots, which are points, and it ignores weather. The notes identify free datasets that fix both problems. Each one can be pulled once by a Python script on a laptop and turned into the static JSON you already ship.

| Dataset | What it adds | Access and licence |
|---|---|---|
| **NEALGEBA** (Landsat 30 m, NE Algeria, 1984-2023) | Real burned perimeters where most forest fires happen; the best training and validation labels | 62 MB of shapefiles on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.10684710); check the licence on the record |
| **MODIS MCD64A1 v6.1** (500 m, monthly, 2000-2025) | National burned-area labels; use v6.1, since v6 was retired in 2023 | Free from LP DAAC with an Earthdata login ([Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-mcd64a1-061)) |
| **VIIRS VNP64A1 v2** (500 m, 2012-present) | Newer burned area; v1 retired July 2025 | [Earthdata alert](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/viirs-version-2-burned-area-data-product-released) |
| **EFFIS** burned areas | Big-fire perimeters, but only fires of about 30 ha or more; historic perimeters need a request form | CC BY 4.0 ([EFFIS data](https://forest-fire.emergency.copernicus.eu/applications/data-and-services)) |
| **CEMS fire danger (FWI)** historical and seasonal | Fire-weather features and a season outlook layer | Free via the Early Warning Data Store since Sept 2024 ([EWDS](https://ewds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/cems-fire-historical-v1)) |
| **CHIRPS3** rainfall | Rainfall for planting suitability and drought; v2 ends after Dec 2026 | [UCSB](https://news.ucsb.edu/2026/022614/new-rainfall-dataset-enhances-global-monitoring-extreme-weather) |
| **ESA WorldCover** (10 m) | "Is this cell forest, shrub or grass", meaning fuel fraction per grid cell | CC BY 4.0, public S3 with no account ([AWS Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/esa-worldcover-vito/)) |
| **Hansen GFC v1.13** (loss to 2025) | Tree-cover baseline and loss year; combined with burn polygons it gives "forest lost to fire" | CC BY 4.0 ([GFW](https://data-api.globalforestwatch.org/dataset/umd_tree_cover_loss)) |
| **Copernicus DEM GLO-30** | Slope, aspect, elevation for both models | Public S3, no account ([AWS Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/)) |
| **SoilGrids 2.0** (250 m) | Soil pH, texture and carbon for planting suitability | CC BY 4.0; cut only the Algeria window from the global files ([ISRIC](https://docs.isric.org/globaldata/soilgrids/SoilGrids_faqs_02.html)) |

The Algerian studies disagree about what drives fire risk. A 2026 northern-Algeria study ranked **wind speed and temperature highest** and roads low ([ESRJ](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/esrj/article/view/119634)). A Zouagha forest study ranked **roads and settlements highest** ([EJFE](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/ejfe/article/1604500)). The difference mostly comes from method: those studies used expert-weighted scoring, not trained models. Your XGBoost pipeline can settle the question for Algeria. Add fire-weather climatology, WorldCover fuel fraction, terrain and distance to roads, then compare against your 0.886 AUC baseline, using a spatially blocked test so the score is not inflated by neighbouring cells. The cheapest daily fire-danger layer is probably to compute FWI yourself from the Open-Meteo forecasts you already use. The notes found no confirmed free daily CEMS forecast feed. Speaking EFFIS's language (FWI classes, burned-area polygons) also makes your output readable to officials, because EFFIS already publishes an Algeria section in its annual Mediterranean report ([EFFIS 2021 report](https://effis.jrc.ec.europa.eu/effis/reports-and-publications/effis-related-publications/Advance_Report_2021_JRC128678.pdf)).

On survival monitoring, set expectations honestly. **Sentinel-2 cannot see individual seedlings.** In one replanting study, recovery happened below what the satellite could detect, while drone NDVI tracked field survival closely (r = 0.918) ([Petrovic et al. 2026](https://iris.univpm.it/retrieve/45becce3-ec3c-44e9-a5c2-7d48b268f287/Petrovic_Climate-adjusted-time-series-analysis_2026.pdf)). Standard NDVI thresholds also fit drylands poorly ([arXiv 2605.04206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04206)). Present satellite data as a "site greening trend" (the planting polygon against a nearby control area, after year 3 or so). Ground photos and check-ins remain the survival truth. The free compute for that trend is **Copernicus Data Space**, which gives 10,000 openEO credits a month ([CDSE](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/3068)).

**Keep Google Earth Engine out of the production pipeline.** Since 27 April 2026, noncommercial projects get monthly quotas (Community tier 150 EECU-hours) ([GEE tiers](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/noncommercial_tiers)). More importantly, individuals **may not be paid for applications or data created with Earth Engine**, and government agencies may not use it free for recurring data products or ongoing services ([Earth Engine noncommercial terms](https://earthengine.google.com/noncommercial)). Today, unpaid and volunteer-run, you qualify. The day you sell to the state, any output "created with Earth Engine" becomes a commercial licence problem. Use GEE only for one-off exploration, and build the repeatable pipeline from open buckets (AWS, LP DAAC, EWDS, CDSE). Your AGPL licence does not change any of this. What matters is whether anyone gets paid.

Algeria's own space agency, ASAL, maps burned forest with Alsat-1B and Alsat-2 imagery and has had a cooperation agreement with the DGF since 2018 ([UN-SPIDER](https://un-spider.org/sites/default/files/12_Conf2021_ASAL_UseOfSpaceTechsToMonitorForestFires.pdf); [Space in Africa](https://spaceinafrica.com/2018/10/15/algeria-space-agency-enters-agreement-to-use-space-technologies-to-monitor-natural-ecosystems/)). There is no public download. Alsat data is a partnership conversation you can only start once you have users and a track record.

## Fix the basemap first; offline comes after you have field users

Your 2,000 to 3,000 points are not the bottleneck. A rough estimate (an inference, not measured) is well under 100 KB gzipped, far below the roughly 5 MB point where vector tiles start to pay off ([rule of thumb](https://skills.cat/skills/mapbox/mapbox-agent-skills/mapbox-web-performance-patterns)). MapLibre's built-in clustering handled 400,000 points in a demo ([Mapbox blog](https://blog.mapbox.com/clustering-millions-of-points-on-a-map-with-supercluster-272046ec5c97)). The weight is the **basemap**. The cheapest win is to **remove the RTL text plugin if you load one**, because MapLibre 6.9.0 and later draws Arabic correctly without it ([MapLibre changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/CHANGELOG.md)); check your version first. Next, **load MapLibre only after the page has painted**, behind a static image placeholder of Algeria. Then **respect Data Saver**: when the `Save-Data` setting is on or the connection is slow ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/optimizing-content-efficiency-save-data)), skip the fire grid, load thumbnails instead of photos, and turn Realtime off. Finally, **use `updateData` instead of `setData`** for Realtime inserts, so the map does not rebuild every cluster on each event.

Next comes a self-hosted Algeria-only Protomaps extract with a capped max zoom. Run `pmtiles extract --dry-run` to see the real size before committing ([Protomaps](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads)). That matters because the public OpenFreeMap instance **has no availability guarantee** ([OpenFreeMap](https://www.github.com/hyperknot/openfreemap)), and a government buyer will ask about uptime. The static 19,000-cell fire-risk grid is the best candidate for a PMTiles file, so the browser only fetches the cells in view. Cloudflare R2 is the documented host. Whether Vercel serves range requests for PMTiles correctly was not verified ([Protomaps deploy](https://docs.protomaps.com/deploy/)).

What breaks first at a national planting day or a big fire is **Supabase Realtime's connection cap: 200 concurrent connections on Free, 500 on Pro** ([Supabase limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits)). Projects that go over can be suspended. Also, `postgres_changes` re-checks access rules once per viewer for every change, on a single thread. For a public map, Supabase recommends **Broadcast fed from a database trigger** ([Supabase benchmarks](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/benchmarks)). That also guarantees `reporter_name` and `reporter_phone` can never leak into a payload. It needs a trigger migration, so under your AGENTS.md rules it waits for your explicit go. A no-schema step you can take right away: open the channel only while the map tab is visible, and refetch on focus when the channel is refused.

For offline field use, the 2026 web recipe is: save a per-wilaya PMTiles file in browser storage (OPFS), let a service worker cache the style, fonts and app shell, and keep an IndexedDB outbox that resends when you are back online, using server-side idempotency keys ([Workbox Background Sync](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/workbox/modules/workbox-background-sync)). An idempotency key is an ID generated on the phone so that a resent form is ignored instead of creating a duplicate. That key is a schema change. Background Sync works in Chrome and Samsung Internet but **not in Android WebView**, so also retry when the app opens. For the Play Store, a **Trusted Web Activity built with Bubblewrap** wraps your existing PWA in about a day and keeps Chrome's offline features ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/using-a-pwa-in-your-android-app)). Capacitor would lose them because it runs in WebView. Running a PWA, a TWA and a separate Expo app at the same time is three surfaces for one person. Ship the TWA, and keep Expo only if field tests prove the web offline path is not enough.

## What a government buyer will check before signing

**Data protection.** Law 18-07, as amended by **Law 25-11 of 24 July 2025**, now looks like GDPR. It requires: a prior declaration to the ANPDP (the data-protection authority) for all processing; authorisation for higher-risk processing and for **transfers abroad**; a **mandatory DPO** (data protection officer); a processing register; an automated log; and **breach notification within five days**. Penalties include imprisonment from two months to five years ([CMS Expert Guide, Jan 2026](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/algeria2)). Green Algeria already processes personal data: fire reporters' names and phones, IP hashes and accounts. Storing them on Supabase and Vercel abroad is a cross-border transfer. That makes it a **compliance gap today**, separate from any sale. One trade-press source says breach notice is 72 hours, but the law-firm reading of the article text (five days) is more reliable. CMS finds no general localisation rule in Law 18-07 itself. However, an ARPT decision requires cloud providers to store customer data in Algeria, and Presidential Decree 25-320 (December 2025) on public-data governance plus the July 2026 National Data Center order make **in-country hosting the practical default for anything a ministry owns** ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/algeria2); [We Are Tech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/algeria-sets-one-month-deadline-for-government-data-center-integration)). No AWS, Azure or Google region exists in Algeria. The local options are Algerie Telecom, ICOSNET and Djezzy ([AlgeriaTech News](https://algeriatech.news/algeria-cloud-colocation-market-djezzy-at-competitive-2026/)). The workable answer is a **"sovereign deployment"** of the same codebase (Postgres/PostGIS plus self-hosted Supabase) on the buyer's own infrastructure. You supply the software and support, and the hosting question becomes theirs. Supabase being open source makes this possible. Vercel-specific features would have to go.

**Language and security.** Law 91-05 makes Arabic the official language of all public administrations, with fines, and it is enforced ([CEFAN](https://axl.cefan.ulaval.ca/afrique/algerie_loi-91.htm)). So the **admin and moderation side must be Arabic-first**, not only the public map. No product certification was found, but Decree 26-07 (January 2026) creates a cybersecurity unit in every public institution ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/algeria2)). Expect that unit to ask for hosting location, access control, logs and ideally a pentest report.

**Procurement.** Law 23-12 (August 2023) governs public purchasing. Its implementing decree was still pending in 2026, so older Decree 15-247 thresholds apply residually: formal tendering below **6M DZD for services** is not mandatory, and below **500,000 DZD** not even consultation is required ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/fra/legal-updates/algerie-nouvelle-reglementation-des-marches-publics-seuils-financiers)). One document lists 8M/4M DZD instead, so verify before quoting. **Article 41 allows a direct negotiated contract ("gre a gre") with labelled startups offering unique digital solutions**. In April 2026, however, the minister said the implementing texts were still being finalised ([Algerie Eco](https://algerie-eco.com/?p=239216)). Donor-funded work follows the donor's rules instead of national procedure ([TESIM factsheet](https://www.interregnextmed.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/TESIM_Factsheet-public-procurement-Algeria.pdf)). The routes, ranked by realism: a **subcontract inside a donor project** (UNDP, World Bank PROGREEN, FAO/GCF, Interreg's MedFireWise); then a **single wilaya conservation or commune pilot under the threshold**; then Article 41 once its texts exist. Every route requires **a legal entity, ideally with the Startup label**. An individual cannot contract with the state. Note that the 2025 Algeria Startup Challenge produced nine agreements, all with companies and none with ministries ([MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/algeria-startup-challenge-2025-forges-nine-corporate-partnerships-to-drive-open-innovation/)).

**The AGPL business model.** AGPL-3.0 lets anyone, including the state, run and modify your code. If they offer a modified version over a network, they must share the source ([GNU AGPL](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html)). So you cannot sell licences. You sell **sovereign deployment, Arabic admin and integration, training, support and optional hosting**. That is a strength with a state buyer (no lock-in, auditable, sovereign), and it makes your moat service, data and relationships. As sole copyright holder you could dual-license, but only if outside contributions come under a contributor agreement. The notes found no Algerian govtech price benchmarks. Tashdjir's USD 872,315 budget covered field reforestation too, so it is not a software price.

## Five strategic risks the notes surfaced

**The existing state platform.** Pitching Green Algeria as the national reforestation tracker puts you in direct competition with UNDP and the DGF's Tashdjir. You will lose that, and it could make DGF staff defensive. Pitch "citizen verification and survival data that feeds Tashdjir and the DGF", with export formats they can load (CSV, GeoJSON, KML; FAO's Open Foris tools are what forest administrations commonly use ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/in-action/openforis/home/open-foris--open-source-solutions-for-forest-and-land-monitoring/en))).

**The name.** The Agriculture Ministry and the DGF co-ran the 25 October 2025 and 14 February 2026 national planting days with the association **"Algerie Verte"**, led by Fouad Maala ([APS](https://www.aps.dz/fr/algerie/sante-et-environnement/mgph08bt-reboisement-campagne-nationale-de-plantation-d-un-million-de-plants-d-arbres-le-25-octobre); [Radio Algerie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/79187)). A separate corporate consortium (BNP Paribas El Djazair, TotalEnergies Lubrifiants Algerie and others) also runs a Sahara forest project called **"Green Algeria"** ([WMC](https://www.webmanagercenter.com/2025/12/24/558547/planter-des-arbres-dans-le-desert-algerien-initiatives-green-algeria-et-relance-du-barrage-vert/)). In French and Arabic your product name translates to the association's name. You have two choices. Either partner openly with Algerie Verte and offer to be the counting and survival tool for its next campaign, which is also the warmest path into the ministry. Or pick a distinct name before any pitch. Drifting in between is the worst option.

**Your current hosting.** As covered above, foreign hosting of personal data without an ANPDP declaration and a lawful transfer basis is a gap now. The cheapest fix is to collect less: make reporter phone optional or drop it. After that, file the declaration, name yourself DPO and publish a privacy notice. Get a short legal check from an Algerian lawyer. The notes are explicitly not legal advice.

**Fire reports must never point at people.** After the 2021 fires, Djamel Bensmail was lynched after being falsely accused of arson, and 49 people were later sentenced to death for his killing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Algeria_wildfires)). On 1 October 2026 the lower house passed a law allowing **the death penalty for forest arson** ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/1/death-penalty-bill-for-forest-arsonists-clears-algeria-lower-house)). Any free-text field, photo or comment that names, describes or locates a suspected arsonist could get someone killed. Fire reports should describe fires only. Moderators should reject anything that suggests a culprit, and that rule should be written into the moderation guide.

**Disputed fire figures.** For August 2026, the DGF reported about **1,666 ha** burned since 20 July, while press reports cited **120,000 to 141,000 ha**. The official toll of 12 dead is contested by local accounts ([LIFE ONG](https://life-ong.org/en-us/actualites/incendies-algerie); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/algerians-battle-deadly-wildfires-as-tebboune-seeks-death-penalty-for-arson-3000450)). Satellite burned-area layers on a public map will produce totals that contradict official ones. That is valuable, but politically sensitive with the very buyer you want. Show satellite data as what it is, with the source, date and method, and do not publish your own national "hectares burned" headline. Let the data speak at the level of individual fires, and offer the comparison privately to partners.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "what features would impress a ministry" to "what does the state lack that it cannot easily build itself". Software it can commission, and already has through UNDP. What it lacks is **independent, method-backed evidence that the trees it pays for survive**, gathered by the citizens and associations it already mobilises for planting days. That is a data asset and a relationship asset, and AGPL code cannot copy either. It also means your first "customer" is probably Algerie Verte or a donor project, not the DGF, and your first proof is a single campaign with published 12-month survival numbers.

The order matters, and it also guards against the "jumping to the next idea" pattern your AGENTS.md warns about. Sort out the name, the data-protection basics and the arson-safety rule before writing any new feature. Then ship survival check-ins before fire-model upgrades or offline mode, because survival is the one thing that makes everything else credible. One honest caveat: several key facts (Tashdjir's status, Article 41's implementing texts, the exact procurement thresholds, Decree 25-320's full scope) rest on secondary sources or remained unverified in the notes. Each deserves one direct phone call or document check before you build a business plan on it.

Next step: email UNDP Algeria and Algerie Verte this week, asking what Tashdjir does today and whether the next national planting day wants a survival-tracking partner. Their answers decide the name, the pitch and the next three months.
